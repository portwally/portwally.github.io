CONQUER GS 2

WHAT'S NEW

Everything in version 1, and a lot more. Click a picture for a closer look.

The campaign

Eight missions against the Red Legion with briefings, goals of their own and new units unlocking as you go. The game remembers how far you got.

New units

Fast light tanks, artillery that out-ranges turrets, flame troopers, and engineers that walk into an enemy power plant, war factory or refinery and take it over. The enemy uses them too.

Bigger maps

Eight maps of 48 x 36 tiles - more than twice the ground of version 1: Forest Run, Islands, Canyon and The Lake join the four from version 1. Units turn to face eight ways, and up to 96 units and structures fight at once.

Map editor

Paint grass, roads, water, trees, rocks and ore, set out your base and the enemy's, place both sides' units, name the map and save it. Start it from the mission setup or from the GS/OS Finder; your maps show up in the skirmish list.

Repair bay

A ninth building on the BASE page: damaged vehicles parked beside it are mended, a few hit points for a few credits, with the wrench blinking. Click it with vehicles selected to send them there.

Rebuild your base

Lose the command center and the game goes on - until every building is gone. Build a new one on its old pad for 1000 credits. It has 800 hit points, and health bars show on any building under the mouse or clicked.

An enemy that fights

It gathers an army at home and sends waves that grow every time, mines with several harvesters, and rebuilds what you destroy - the last missions on hard are tougher than hard.

Rally flags and icons

Setting a rally point turns the cursor into a flag and plants one on the spot. The game and the map editor show the Iron Guard's fist in the GS/OS Finder.

Settings

Music and effects volume, scroll speed, mouse speed and three colour schemes - standard, desert and high contrast - kept on the disk for next time.

Saved games and groups

Three save slots from the pause menu, unit groups on Control-1 to 9, and five music tracks on the Ensoniq chip.

What you need

An Apple IIgs (ROM 01 or ROM 3) with at least 2 MB of memory and a mouse - or MAME. One 800K ProDOS disk image, the map editor on it.

THE ENEMY

THREAT: HIGH

The enemy has a base like yours, mines ore with a harvester of its own, and sends its army against you in waves that grow bigger over time. It rebuilds what you destroy once it has the money - so hit it where it hurts, and keep hitting:

  • Destroy its harvester - no money until its refinery has made a new one, 30 seconds later.
  • Destroy its war factory - no more tanks and buggies.
  • Destroy its refinery - no more money.
  • Destroy its power plant - it builds at half speed.

STANDING ORDERS

  1. 1Keep the ore flowing. Your harvester mines on its own: 25 credits a scoop, 500 a load. Each ore tile holds 600, and mined-out ore slowly grows back. A refinery near the ore shortens its trips.
  2. 2Barracks first. You start with a command center only: a barracks lets you build a war factory, and the war factory your tanks and buggies. Harvesters come from the command center. Infantry, vehicles, harvesters and structures build in four queues, all at the same time.
  3. 3Mind the power. Your command center makes 100, each power plant 100 more. Too little, and everything slows down.
  4. 4Scout ahead. The fog hides the enemy until one of your units has seen it.
  5. 5Defend early. A couple of turrets and guard towers by the command center hold off the first waves.
  6. 6Match your weapons. Rockets against tanks and buildings, machine guns against soldiers.
  7. 7Finish the job. You win when every enemy unit and building is gone. The debriefing shows units built and lost, enemies destroyed, ore mined and your time.

UNDER THE HOOD

65C816 @ 2.8 MHZ

Pure 65816 assembly

No compiler, no engine: every routine written by hand for the IIgs's 16-bit CPU and assembled with Merlin 32.

60 frames a second

Sprites are compiled into code, only changed screen cells are redrawn, and the view is copied with the stack - fast enough for 48 units fighting at once.

A world twice the screen

Four maps that scroll in every direction - Green Valley, River Crossing, Twin Lakes and Stone Pass - with fog of war, pathfinding around rivers, lakes and rocky ridges, and a radar.

Ensoniq sound

Recorded weapon sounds and two original music tracks played on the IIgs's wavetable sound chip, in time even while the view scrolls.

Original pixel art

Hand-drawn units and buildings modeled in tiny 3D, all in the 16 colors of Super Hi-Res.

Boots from ProDOS

One 800K disk: the program, its packed graphics, the sounds and the title picture.
