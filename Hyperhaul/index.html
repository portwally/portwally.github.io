APPLE IIGS
Space trading and combat for the Apple IIgs

Eight galaxies. One ship.

Trade grain for gold and computers for credits. Hunt pirates for their bounties, or run guns for the warlords and stay a jump ahead of the police. Earn your rating from Greenhorn to Legend, and when the Guild calls, answer. Hyperhaul is a new game in the spirit of the first Elite, written for the Apple IIgs in 65816 assembly, its ships drawn as filled, shaded polygons in real time.

What you do

Trade, fight, haul

You start docked at the station of Kaniveda with a Kestrel freighter, a pulse laser and a hundred credits. What you make of it is up to you.

TradeBuy low, sell high

Every system has its economy, from rich forge worlds to poor frontier farms, and its market follows it. Seventeen goods, two of them illegal; prices that move with every visit.

FightPirates and bounties

Pulse, beam, mining and military lasers on four mounts, homing missiles, an E.C.M., a nova charge. Every kill counts towards your rating; pirates carry a price on their heads.

Explore2,048 worlds

Eight charts of 256 systems, each with its planet, people, government and tech level, generated by the game as the first Elite did. Hyperspace takes you there; a mis-jump drops you among the Shard.

Drawn in real time

On the Ensoniq

The music

Two original tunes, synthesized for the IIgs's Ensoniq sound chip: nothing sampled. These recordings are the chip's own sound, rendered from the game's music data. As in the first Elite, there is no music in flight, except while the docking computer flies you in.

The title screenOverdrive

Rock in E minor at 150: chugging power chords, a saw bass, a rock beat and a screaming lead.

The docking computerOrbit Waltz

D major, 3/4: a lilting lead with its echo, plucked chords, the bass on the first beat.

How it is made

Inside

Filled polygons in fill mode

The IIgs's Super Hi-Res screen can fill a line's colour by itself to the next change. Hyperhaul draws only the edges of its shaded ships, and the hardware fills them in: the vector engine of FILLHARMONIC, grown into a game.

65816 assembly

About 18,000 lines of assembly for Merlin 32: the renderer, the flight model, the galaxy generator, the screens, the music player. The game runs from one 800K disk in 1 MB.

Checked byte for byte

A Python model of the whole game runs beside it. Every test scene, over 700 frames of flight, combat, docking and screens, is played in the emulator and compared with the model to the last byte of the screen and the game's state.

A galaxy from seeds

The 2,048 systems, their names, economies, governments and markets are generated by the game from three seeds, as the first Elite did, so the disk holds no star charts at all.

Sound on the Ensoniq

Ten sound effects and two tunes, all synthesized in Python and laid out in the chip's 64K of sound memory; the music player runs in the video interrupt.

Its own content

The rules follow the first Elite (Ian Bell and David Braben, 1984), as explained in Mark Moxon's annotated source. The ships, galaxies, names, words, pictures and music are Hyperhaul's own.
