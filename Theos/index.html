Theos

A god game for the Apple IIgs

You are a god of Olympus. You can't build a hut or swing a sword, but you can move the earth itself: raise the land out of the sea, level the hills, and your people settle on it, build and multiply. Across the world the Titans' people do the same, and their god has every one of your gifts too.

Α

You move the earth

They do the rest

A world is 64 by 64 squares of hills in the sea, seen from above at an angle. You shape it; your people decide for themselves what to make of it.

  1. Shape the land

    A click raises the corner under the pointer, Option-click lowers it, and its neighbours follow. Your people build only where the ground is flat, so you level it for them, a step at a time.

  2. They settle and grow

    Walkers look for flat, empty land and pitch a tent. With room around it a tent becomes a hut, a farmhouse, a villa, a temple, at last a citadel. Full homes send out new walkers, and every home gives you mana.

  3. Gather and march

    Light the Sacred Flame and your people gather round their Oracle. Give the order to march, and a band of 32 or more lays siege to the Titans' homes. The world is yours when no Titan is left on it.

Your people

From a tent to a citadel

Each time its turn comes round, a home counts the flat, free squares among the 24 around it, and grows or shrinks to match. Bigger homes hold more people, give more mana, and are far harder to take.

Under each home: the flat, free squares it needs, and how many people it holds.

Four orders

You don't steer anyone. You tell your whole people what to be about, and each walker does it as best it can.

  • Settle S
    Spread out, find flat land, build homes.
  • Gather G
    Go to the Sacred Flame and join the Oracle's band.
  • March M
    Seek the enemy: fight his walkers, besiege his homes.
  • Join J
    Merge into fewer, stronger bands.

How they live

  • A full home sends half of its people out as a new walker.
  • A walker who meets an enemy fights; the stronger band wins.
  • The first walker to reach the Sacred Flame is your Oracle; those who come after join its band.
  • A marching band of 32 or more takes an enemy home that holds no more than half as many people.
  • No one builds within three squares of the other tribe's homes.

Nine divine powers

The panel's icons, and what each costs in mana

Mana comes from your people's homes. Spend it on the land, or save it for something the Titans will remember. An icon lights up when the gold bar reaches its mark.

    The last battle: every home empties, and everyone fights until one people is left.

Δ

The Titans and their god

He plays by your rules

Across the world a second people settles, in red, and a second god shapes the land.

He levels the ground round his homes, a corner at a time. He lights his own Sacred Flame by the home nearest to yours, gathers a band, and marches: when they set out, you hear their horn. What mana he doesn't spend on the land he saves for one power after another, and casts each where it hurts most.

Every world's tablet tells you what kind of god you face. A mild god thinks slowly, and in the first eight worlds starts no war at all until you take a home of his. A fierce god is at your border in three minutes and casts a power every 50 seconds, if he has the mana.

Custom worlds

Put a world together yourself: any of the four landscapes, one of 256 maps, the plain you start on (wide, fair, narrow or bare), the Titans' god (mild, steady, bold or fierce), and the powers: all nine for both gods, none but shaping and the flame, or by the god's rank.

Sound and music

Sixteen sound effects and six tunes on the IIgs's Ensoniq sound chip: lyre, reed pipe, pan flute and frame drum, in the old modes. Thunder rolls over the title screen, and the Titans' horn warns you when they march.
