Real-time strategy for the Apple IIgs

Three armies.One desert.

The Order, the Defence Force and the Desert Riders all want the same sand and the oil under it. Each has its own nine units, its own ten buildings and a special power of its own — and no two of them fight alike. Written by hand in 65816 assembly.

Choose your side

The three armies

Nine units, ten buildings and one special power each — none of them shared. Play any of the three: there is no villain here, only three armies with different strengths. Pick one to see its roster.

The Order

Stone walls, heavy armour

Slow to build, dear to pay for and very hard to kill. The Order digs in behind stone walls and castle towers, mends its soldiers in the hospital and in the field, and breaks a siege with the heaviest tank in the desert.

  • The toughest soldiers and the heaviest tank
  • Stone walls, castle towers, archer posts
  • Field surgeons and a hospital heal the infantry

The Defence Force

Long reach, high tech

Few units, each of them dear, most of them out-reaching their rivals. The Defence Force sees first, shoots first, mines the approaches and shoots enemy shells out of the air.

  • Snipers, howitzers and the longest guns
  • A recon drone that flies over everything
  • Mines, and interceptors against artillery

The Desert Riders

Fast, many, unseen

Cheap, quick and everywhere at once. The Riders have no tanks and no power plants: they have camels, rocket buggies, tunnels under the sand, nets that hide a base, and the men to shut yours down.

  • The cheapest, fastest units in the game
  • Tunnels, camouflage nets and saboteurs
  • No power needed; scavengers strip the wrecks

How a battle goes

In the field

Build a base, bring in the oil, raise an army, find the others before they find you.

Oil pays for everything

Tankers carry the oil from the fields to your refinery and go back for more. The Desert Riders send workers with cans instead. Lose them and the money stops.

A war for three

Skirmish against one computer army or two — every army for itself, or the two of them allied against you — at easy, normal or hard. Each computer army plays its own style.

Special powers

The Charge, the Airstrike, the Sandstorm: each army's power charges while you fight. Use it when it counts — the computer uses its own.

Fog of war

The map is dark until you have been there. A bell tower, an intel centre or a watchtower gives you the radar: the whole map in the corner of the screen.

Take it, break it, strip it

An engineer walks into an enemy building and it is yours. The Riders' saboteur shuts one down instead, and their scavengers strip the wrecks for credits.

Walls, mines, tunnels

Stone and mud walls close a road. The Defence Force's mines wait under the sand. The Riders go in at one cave and come out of another — and hide a base under nets.

Survival

No end and no mercy: the enemy comes in waves and every wave is bigger. The game keeps your best time.

Yours to command

All by mouse, like the classics: drag a box, click to move or attack, groups on Control-1 to 9, rally flags for new units, three save slots.

Straight from the game

Screenshots

320 by 200 pixels, 16 colours, as the Apple IIgs shows them. Each army brings its own sidebar: limestone and red, steel and blue, leather and green. Click one for a closer look.

64 by 48 tiles each

Sixteen battlefields

Sand, roads, palms, rock, water and oil. Every map has room for three bases — and the map editor makes more.

Eight tunes, nothing sampled

The music

Every note is synthesized on the Apple IIgs's Ensoniq chip: lute, qanun, reed flute, zurna and hand drums on seven voices. These are recordings of the game itself.

The title tune plays on the title and through the menus. In a battle the other seven take turns, starting with your army's own; Control-T shows the tune's name.

On the same disk

The map editor

Paint sand, roads, water, palms, rocks and oil. Set out your base, then the bases of the first and the second computer army, and place their units. Name the map, save it, and it shows up in the skirmish list.

An overview shows the whole map at once, and a saved map can be loaded again to keep working on it.

Under the hood

Inside

No engine, no compiler. The game is built on the engine of Conquer GS 2, taken apart and put together again for three sides.

Pure 65816 assembly

Every routine written by hand for the IIgs's 16-bit processor and assembled with Merlin 32.

Sprites compiled into code

Units, buildings and map tiles become machine code that draws them, and only the cells that changed are drawn again: close to 60 frames a second in a battle.

Three armies in memory

Three complete sets of units and buildings do not fit in 2 MB. The game asks for an Apple IIgs with 4 MB.

Up to 96 at once

Units and buildings of all three sides, each finding its own way round rocks, water and walls on maps of 64 by 48 tiles.

A music player of its own

Seven voices on ten of the Ensoniq's oscillators, with envelopes, vibrato, trills and grace notes. The other 22 play the sound effects.

A style per army

The computer builds and attacks by its army's own plan: heavy and patient, long-ranged, or fast and many.

Tested by the machine

About ninety scripts play the game in the MAME emulator after every change and check what happens.

One 800K disk

The game, its three armies, sixteen maps, the music and the map editor boot from a single 3.5-inch ProDOS disk.
