TileTown 2 · for the Apple IIgsThe City Rises Into the Third Dimension

Buildings stand up from the map, drawn in 32 × 16 diamonds - and cars, trains and tornadoes now pass behind them

TileTown, February 1900. - The city has stood up. In TileTown 2, homes, shops, factories and power plants rise from the map in an isometric view, drawn in 32 × 16 diamonds. Cars, trains and even tornadoes go behind the buildings, not over them.

TileTown 2 is the sequel to TileTown, the 2 MB original. It was written from scratch in 65816 assembly by Walter Tengler, 2026, for the Apple IIgs.

Everything from the first town is still here - the 128 × 128 map, 16 maps to start on, 17 things to build, five disasters and five goals - and there is far more to look at on every street. Town news, page 2.

Four Designs on Every Street

No two streets look alike

Every house, shop and factory comes in four designs, and which one appears is picked by where it stands. Build a row of homes and you get a street, not a row of copies.

Traffic Faces the Right Way

Trains keep to the rails

Cars, buses and vans face the way they drive, and trains run on the rails. The commuters still drive to work and back, as they did in the original - now seen from an angle.

A Living Picture

Lakes ripple, chimneys smoke

Look closely and the town is never still. Lakes ripple, chimneys smoke and the nuclear plant steams. The airport's beacon flashes, and over at the stadium the score goes up.

Smooth Scrolling Arrives

A few pixels at a time

Move across the city and the map slides smoothly, a few pixels at a time, from the seaport to the factories and back.

It all runs on the Apple IIgs itself, with an accelerator: TileTown 2 runs from 8 MHz and is at its best at 16 MHz. What you need, page 2.

Town News

Press I for the Old Map

The flat, top-down view is one key away

Miss the old view? Press I and the city lies flat again, seen from straight above as in the original TileTown. Press I once more and the buildings stand back up.

Both views show the same town, with the same toolbox, menus and prices, so you can plan in one and admire the result in the other.

The Old Town’s Ways All Return

Everything from TileTown, too

A living city. On a 128 × 128 map, homes, shops and factories grow in three sizes once they have power, a road and people who want them. Commuters drive to work and back.

17 things to build. Roads, rail, power lines, homes, shops, industry, coal and nuclear power plants, police and fire stations, hospitals, schools, parks, a stadium, a seaport and an airport. Bridges over water, level crossings, and rail tunnels under lakes and rivers.

Neighbourhoods. Land value, traffic, pollution and crime spread through the neighbourhoods, and people ask for what the city lacks.

Maps and graphs. Heat maps for pollution, crime, land value, traffic, police, fire, health, schools, population and power; graphs over 10 or 100 years.

The budget. Sliders for taxes, roads, police and fire.

Advice. Milestones and monthly advice, a city evaluation with a score, and a query tool.

At the controls. Menus, pause and three speeds, a help screen, mouse and keyboard.

Saving. Eight save slots, each city under its own name.

Music. Sound effects and eight relaxed tunes on the Ensoniq sound chip.

Sixteen Sites Offered to New Mayors

Rivers, lakes, coasts, islands, forests, fjords, marshes and plains

Every new city starts on one of 16 maps. Pick a map, then a goal and a level, and the land is yours to lay out. Bridges cross the water, roads and rails meet at level crossings, and rail tunnels run under the lakes and rivers.

What You Need

  • An Apple IIgs with 8 MB of memory.
  • A drive that boots an 800K 3.5" ProDOS disk.
  • An accelerator (ZipGS, TransWarp GS or similar): it runs from 8 MHz and is at its best at 16 MHz.
  • Or an emulator such as MAME (on the Mac: Ample, or EmuBuddy), set up as an Apple IIgs with 8 MB and the CPU type “16 MHz ZipGS”.
  • A mouse is easier; the keyboard works too.
