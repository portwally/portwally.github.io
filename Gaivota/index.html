Fly out to the smoke. Bring them home.

GAIVOTA is a helicopter rescue game for the Apple IIgs, flown over a map of mainland Portugal built from OpenStreetMap. Surfers in the big waves at Nazaré, a fishing boat sinking off Peniche, a fire in the pines of Leiria, Porto's roofs in a flood: hover low and slow to winch them up, then land softly at the base before the clock runs out.

How a rescue goes

Out, down, and home

The arrow on the panel points the way; the smoke rises over the people. Three lamps tell you when.

TGT 1.5KM

Fly out to the smoke

The arrow points to the nearest people while a seat is free, and their smoke rises over them. Up to 252 km/h, and up to 2,000 m; the brief tells you where.

WINCH

Hover low and slow

Come down by the smoke and slow to a hover: WINCH lights up, the cable goes down and they come up one at a time. Six seats; Porto has ten on its roofs.

LAND

Land softly at the base

Near the base LAND lights up. Touch down gently, under 5 m/s down and 12 m/s forward, and they get out. Soft on the water and you ditch; hard, and you crash.

The panel

Everything you need, at a glance: drawn by the game below its 3D view, here in Porto with one man aboard and the winch working.

123 456 789
  1. Airspeed, 0 to 300 km/h.
  2. Radar altitude, 0 to 500 m; the red mark at 25 m is the winch height.
  3. The compass: your heading under the blue mark, the objective under the orange one.
  4. The objective: TGT the people, BASE where to land them, HOME in free flight, and how far.
  5. The arrow to the objective.
  6. Aboard, and saved: ON 1, 00/10 (FLYING or LANDED in free flight).
  7. Climb and sink, up to 10 m/s.
  8. WINCH, LAND, LOW: steady when it works, blinking when you are close but too high or fast; LOW warns of a hard landing.
  9. The clock, the time left or flown, and the mission's name.

Before you fly

What you need, and the keys

What you need

  • An Apple IIgs with 8 MB of RAM: the whole map lives in memory.
  • A hard disk: the game is a 32 MB ProDOS hard-disk image.
  • A 16 MHz accelerator (ZipGS, TransWarp GS) is best. At stock speed it runs, slowly: 4 to 6 frames a second, and about 90 seconds to load.
  • ProDOS 8 or GS/OS 6: from GS/OS it asks the Memory Manager for its memory, and quits back to the Finder.
  • In MAME (Ample on the Mac): 8 MB, a 16 MHz ZipGS, the disk on a CFFA card in slot 7.
  • The keyboard, or a joystick.

How it is made

Inside

Portugal from OpenStreetMap

Python tools turn the Geofabrik extract of Portugal into a 300 by 600 km map: coast, rivers and lakes, beaches, forests, and towns from the density of their buildings, in three levels of detail. 4.6 MB of it sits in memory.

Filled 3D in fill mode

Every edge is turned, clipped and projected into one colour change per screen line, and the Super Hi-Res fill mode paints the rest: the fill engine of the PORTUGAL demo, grown into a flight game.

Landmarks, solid

Boxes at any heading, arches and cables, from OSM outlines and heights; drawn far to near over the ground, simpler far away, and checked against the helicopter every frame.

65816 assembly

About 15,000 lines for Merlin 32: the renderer, the flight model, the missions, the panel, the music player. The landmarks are their own module, in a bank of their own.

Sound on the Ensoniq

The rotor follows the collective and your speed; the music and every effect are synthesized in Python and laid out in the chip's 64K of sound memory.

Flown by scripts

Headless MAME runs, driven by Lua scripts, flew every mission, crashed into every landmark and checked the landmark views against a Python model of the renderer.
